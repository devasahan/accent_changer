4 · Listen and rate

    Score: – / 10  (a line counts when all four boxes are ticked; “–” until you tick a box)

    Blind listener: a friend who knows your voice, and did not hear you read, listens to lines of this run with the small ▶ converted buttons, without seeing the script, and says what they heard. Each run gets different lines, so no sentence is heard twice: lines 1–5 for the first run you play, 6–10 for the second, 11–15 for the third.

    5 · Live: hear yourself converted

    Your converted voice plays in your headphones, a little late. That feels strange; it is normal.

    6 · Stability: one long connection

    Plays your recording again and again through one Sanas connection: 10 minutes cost about $0.09, 65 minutes about $0.60. If the connection drops, the page reconnects and counts the drop. Leave the PC awake and plugged in.

    7 · Measure another app's microphone (Krisp, the Sanas desktop app, VB-CABLE)

    Pick that app's microphone, for example “Krisp Microphone”. The page records your headset microphone and that one at the same time and compares them. In the other app, use your headset as its microphone.

    Read the script: press the button, read the whole script from step 2 aloud with about one second of pause between lines (as in step 2), then press Stop. The page listens 3 more seconds for the app's last words. The result appears in step 3's table and in step 4 for rating, like a Sanas run. If step 4 shows far fewer lines than your script has, read it again with longer pauses.

    Long check: start it, then hold a full-length test call that uses the app (as in your interview), including a 15-minute stretch where you stay silent. It follows your microphone and the app's for up to 90 minutes, keeping only the last few seconds, and notes the delay and whether the app comes back at once after the silence.

    Quick check: 12 seconds of “pa-ta-ka” with pauses; measures the delay only. With “CABLE Output” the page plays your microphone into VB-CABLE first and measures the cable.

    8 · Save

    The JSON is the file to share. It has no audio: it holds the measurements, your ratings and notes, the settings with your device names, the browser version, whose voice it is, the script text only if you changed it, the Sanas address check, and the page log. WAV files are your voice: keep them on your PC.
